# Packs and Cinematics Implementation Plan (Plan 2 of 3)

> **Status: as-built.** This plan was written after a working spike. The risky parts (glb/wav/hdr writers, sequencer timing, motion math, driver, scene glue) were built and verified first, so the plan records what landed, task by task, one commit per task. Each task lists its files, the interfaces neighbouring tasks rely on, what its tests assert, and its commit message. Code lives in the commits, not in this document.

**Goal:** Replace Plan 1's instant moves and primitive pieces with content packs, a sequencer, and skippable capture cinematics, so a game plays on the Angels vs Demons set and the stone-and-lava board with animated moves, sound, particles, and bloom.

**Architecture:** Packs are manifests plus glb/audio/hdr files loaded by URL, validated at load, with built-in fallbacks. The controller still owns all game state and still only signals `animationDone()`. A plain-TypeScript sequencer plays data-only sequences against imperative handles (pieces, camera, effects, audio) held in a registry; React scene components register their handles on mount. A driver watches the controller phase, builds the right sequence, and calls `animationDone()` when it finishes, or skips it when the phase changes underneath it. Pure logic (sequences, motion, particles, audio, assets, validation) is unit tested with fake clocks and recording handles; glue is checked by an e2e suite that asserts the drawn scene matches the controller.

**Tech Stack:** As Plan 1, plus `@react-three/postprocessing` and `postprocessing` (bloom, vignette, tone mapping), `tsx` (dev pack generator), `@types/node`.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-19-3d-chess-simulator-design.md` (sections 4.4 to 4.6, 5, 6, 7, 10, 11, 12)

**Follow-on plan:** Plan 3 (art pipeline). It replaces the procedural dev packs under `public/packs/` with generated art and adds `tools/normalize.ts`. See "Hand-off to Plan 3".

## Global Constraints

- Node 20 or newer. Package manager is pnpm. TypeScript strict mode everywhere, including tests and `tools/`.
- Game core has no React, Three.js, or DOM imports. Controller is the only code that calls `GameCore.move`.
- One square is 1.0 world units wide. Board centered at the origin; `a1` at x = -3.5, z = 3.5 (white's side is +z).
- Piece heights: king 1.0, queen 0.9, bishop and knight 0.75, rook 0.7, pawn 0.55.
- Model conventions: Y-up, base at y = 0, centered on x and z, under 2 MB per file, clips named exactly `idle`, `attack`, `hit`, `die`, `victory`, `idle` loops cleanly. Black pieces are rotated 180 degrees by the engine, not baked in.
- `impactEffect` is one of `light`, `fire`, `ice`, `shadow`, `sparks`. Board `ambient.type` is one of `none`, `lava-cracks`.
- Board manifest square colors: light `#8c8378`, dark `#3b3733`, highlight `#ffd66b`, capture `#ff5a3c`.
- Default capture sequence, total 3900 ms: camera fly 0 (900 ms); attacker `attack` and move to the defender's square minus 0.5 units at 700 (600 ms); defender `hit`, burst, camera shake (250 ms), hit sound at 1200; defender `die` and fade (700 ms), die sound at 1500; attacker `victory` at 2300; attacker moves onto the captured square at 2900 (400 ms); camera returns at 3000 (900 ms).
- Non-capture move: 350 ms slide. Castling slides both pieces at once. Check pulses the king's material for one second. Checkmate: slow 6 s orbit while the losing king dies.
- Rigid fallback clips: idle bob 2 mm amplitude and 3 s period; attack lunge 0.4 units and back over 600 ms with ease-out; hit 80 ms recoil plus red flash; die scale to zero over 500 ms with a shatter burst; victory one quick hop.
- A model that fails to load renders as a placeholder with one console warning per model. A missing clip uses the rigid fallback.
- Audio blocked by the autoplay policy stays muted until the first click, with no error. WebGL context loss shows an overlay asking to reload.
- Sequencer tested with a fake clock. Commit after every task with a conventional-commit message.

## Decisions Where the Spec Is Silent or Ambiguous

1. **Dev packs are procedural.** Lathe-profile pieces, a slab-and-frame board with a `cracks` mesh, a sky HDR, and synthesized WAVs are generated by `pnpm dev-packs` and committed under `public/packs/`. They satisfy every model convention, so Plan 3's real art is a drop-in replacement. Sounds are `.wav` rather than the spec's `.ogg` because the manifest paths are free-form and a WAV is trivial to synthesize deterministically.
2. **Forward direction.** The spec says models face +Z and that "forward" means toward the opponent, which contradict for white (whose side is +z). Plan 1's rule stands: black pieces are rotated by pi, white are not. Rigid clips do not use model orientation: `attack` lunges along the direction of the piece's latest move, `hit` recoils opposite it, and the default aim is toward the opponent's side in world space.
3. **Hold and finish.** The controller commits a move's result to state immediately, so a piece's props already show the destination when its animation starts. A sequence therefore calls `hold()` on every piece it touches (the piece keeps its pre-move pose and ignores prop changes), and `finish()` at the end or on skip (snap tweens to their ends, release the hold, adopt the controller's square and type). `reset()` restores everything the controller says, including visibility. A promoted pawn keeps its pawn model until `finish()`.
4. **Captured pieces stay mounted** so the fade can play and undo can restore them. Visibility comes from the `captured` flag.
5. **Check pulse runs in parallel** with the slide and lasts one second, so a checking move holds the turn for one second instead of 350 ms. The mating move plays the pulse before the game-over banner.
6. **Checkmate orbit** starts when the phase becomes `gameOver` with reason `checkmate`. It does not block the controller. The banner shows immediately. Leaving `gameOver` (new game or undo) skips it and resets every handle, which brings the faded losing king back.
7. **Squares are drawn at 0.94 of a unit** so the grooves between them show the lava cracks beneath. The pack's `cracks` mesh is the only thing that gets the shader.
8. **Loading never throws.** `src/scene/assets.ts` is a small cache with a `use(url)` hook returning the asset or null. React error boundaries around Suspense re-throw load errors as uncaught page errors, which the spec's "one console warning" rules out. Failures are warned once per URL and never retried.
9. **Manifest failure falls back.** If a manifest is missing or invalid, the built-in fallback set (placeholders in the side colors) or board (Plan 1 look) is used, with one warning. The app starts on the fallbacks and swaps in the real packs when they arrive, so the board is visible before any pack loads.
10. **Scene registry.** The sequencer holds one stable `SequencerHandles` object. Components register and unregister handles in a `SceneRegistry`; unknown pieces resolve to no-ops so a piece that is still mounting can never break a sequence. Each unregister removes only its own handle.

### Task 2: Sequencer core

**Files:**
- Create: `src/sequencer/{types,sequencer,testing}.ts`, `src/sequencer/sequencer.test.ts`

**Interfaces:**
- Produces (all in `types.ts`): `Step` (discriminated union of `camera.flyTo | camera.shake | camera.restore | camera.orbit | piece.play | piece.moveTo | piece.fadeOut | piece.pulse | effects.burst | audio.play`, each with `at` and `duration` in ms), `Sequence { steps: Step[] }`, `PieceHandle { play, moveTo, fadeOut, pulse, hold, finish, reset, snapshot }`, `CameraHandle { flyTo, shake, restore, orbit, finish, reset }`, `EffectsHandle { burst }`, `AudioHandle { play }`, `SequencerHandles { piece(id), camera, effects, audio, reset() }`, `Clock { setTimeout, clearTimeout }`, `Sequencer { run(sequence, handles) => { done, skip } }`, plus `Vec3`, `Point2`, `CameraPose`, `PieceSnapshot`.
- Produces: `createSequencer(clock = realClock)`, `sequenceDuration(sequence)`, `realClock`.
- Test helpers: `createFakeClock()` (manual `advance(ms)`, equal deadlines fire in scheduling order), `createRecordingHandles(clock)` returning `{ handles, calls }`.

**Behaviour:** `run` calls `hold()` on every piece a sequence touches, applies `at <= 0` steps synchronously, schedules the rest, and completes at `sequenceDuration`, calling `finish()` on every touched piece handle and on the camera if used, then resolving `done`. `skip()` applies unstarted steps with zero duration (dropping sounds, particles, clips and pulses), then completes. A throwing handle is logged and never stalls the sequence.

**Tests assert:** stable step ordering at equal offsets; exact fire times; `done` resolves at the end with every touched handle finished; skip semantics and idempotence, including no-op after natural completion; nothing fires after skip; a throwing handle does not stall.

**Commit:** `feat(sequencer): add timed-step sequencer with fake clock and skip`

## Deferred, Known Gaps

- The quality setting that turns bloom off automatically when the first second of frames is slow (spec section 15). Bloom is always on.
- `preview.png` in each pack (only the Version B selector needs it).
- The `sequenceOverride` manifest field is validated as `null` and otherwise unsupported.
- Draco-compressed models load their decoder from the gstatic CDN (three's default path). Plan 3 should decide whether to self-host it once the normalizer chooses a compression.
- The production JavaScript bundle is 1.4 MB (about 395 KB gzipped) in one chunk. Well inside the 15 MB first-load budget; splitting is optional.

## Hand-off to Plan 3 (art pipeline)

Plan 3 replaces the procedural files and nothing else:
